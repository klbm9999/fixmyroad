## What is required

- **Speed limit signs** must be posted where a limit is in force so that drivers know the applicable limit. Default limits may apply in the absence of signs (e.g. in built-up areas), but signed limits are clearer and enforceable.
- Signs should be **visible** (correct size, not faded or obscured) and **repeated** where necessary (e.g. after junctions).
- As per the Motor Vehicles Act and state rules, speed limits are set by the competent authority and enforced by traffic police / RTO.

## What is often missing or wrong

- **No speed limit sign** on stretches where a limit is intended (e.g. near schools, residential areas, or accident-prone spots).
- **Illegible or damaged** speed limit signs.
- **Signs obscured** by vegetation or other structures.
- **Conflicting or outdated** signs (e.g. old limit not removed after a change).

If you see a road where a speed limit should be clearly posted but the sign is missing or unreadable, that is a gap worth reporting.

## Who to report to

- **Installation and maintenance of speed limit signs** on city roads: **GHMC** (or the road-owning agency) — [GHMC complaint portal](/knowledge/ghmc/how-to-lodge-complaint), helpline **21111111**.
- **Enforcement and rules**: **RTO / Transport Department** — [RTO contacts](/knowledge/rto/rto-contacts). Reporting a missing or illegible sign to GHMC helps get the sign fixed; the road authority and RTO work together on speed management.
