## What is required

As per [IRC (Indian Road Congress)](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.073.2018.pdf) and traffic guidelines, the following markings are expected on roads where traffic flow and safety warrant them:

- **Centre line**: A continuous or broken white line along the centre of the carriageway to separate opposing traffic. On two-lane roads, a broken centre line is standard; solid where overtaking is restricted.
- **Edge line**: Lines along the left (and where applicable, right) edge of the carriageway to define the drivable area and improve night visibility.
- **Lane lines**: On multi-lane roads, white lines separating lanes for same-direction traffic.
- **Pedestrian and other crossings**: See [Crossings](/knowledge/checklist/crossings) for zebra crossings, stop lines, and non-pedestrian crossings (e.g. railway, cattle).
- **Stop lines**: Solid white lines at signalised intersections and before pedestrian crossings.

Markings should be clearly visible (retro-reflective where possible) and maintained so they do not fade or disappear.

## What is often missing or wrong

- **Faded or missing centre line** on two-lane roads, making it hard to judge overtaking.
- **No edge lines** on many internal and even some major roads, so the carriageway boundary is unclear at night or in rain.
- **Missing or worn zebra crossings** at busy pedestrian points.
- **No stop lines** at junctions, or lines too far back from the crossing.
- **Wrong type or spacing** of lines (e.g. solid where broken is required, or inconsistent with IRC dimensions).

If you see a road with no centre/edge markings where traffic is significant, or crossings that are faded or absent at pedestrian-heavy locations, that is a maintenance gap worth reporting.

## Who to report to

**GHMC** (or the road-owning agency for that stretch) is responsible for road markings on municipal and most city roads in Hyderabad. Report missing or damaged lane markings through the [GHMC complaint portal](/knowledge/ghmc/how-to-lodge-complaint) or helpline **21111111**.
