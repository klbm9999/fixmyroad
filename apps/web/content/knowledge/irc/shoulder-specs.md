IRC (Indian Road Congress) documents, including **IRC:SP-73** (Second Revision, 2018) for two-lane highways with paved shoulder, specify requirements for shoulders and margins. See [IRC:SP-73 (2018)](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.073.2018.pdf) for width and surface specifications.

## Shoulder width and function

- **Width**: Shoulder width is specified in the relevant IRC code for the road type (e.g. state highways, urban roads). It should be sufficient for vehicles to pull off safely and for drainage.
- **Function**: Shoulders provide lateral support to the pavement, space for emergency stop, and often carry utilities. They should be **stable** and **trafficable** where vehicles might need to use them.

## Surface type

- Shoulders may be **paved** (bituminous or concrete) or **unpaved** (stabilised or gravel) depending on the road category and IRC recommendations.
- **Paved shoulders** improve ride quality and drainage and reduce edge failure of the main pavement. They should be maintained so that they do not break up or erode.
- **Unpaved shoulders** should be properly shaped and drained and free of potholes and erosion.

## Integration with carriageway

- The **transition** between carriageway and shoulder should be even (no step or sharp drop) so that vehicles can move on and off safely.
- **Edge treatment** (e.g. edge line, rumble strip) may be used as per traffic and safety guidelines.

## Maintenance

- Shoulders should be **cleared of encroachments** and **repaired** when damaged (potholes, erosion, subsidence) so that they continue to serve their function.

Exact shoulder widths (e.g. **2.5 m** paved shoulder for two-lane highways as per IRC:SP-73) and surface specifications are in [IRC:SP-73 (2018)](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.073.2018.pdf). The date above refers to the IRC document revision.
