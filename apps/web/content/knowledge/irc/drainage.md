IRC (Indian Road Congress) guidelines require adequate drainage for roads so that water does not damage the pavement or cause unsafe conditions. See [IRC:SP-73 (2018)](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.073.2018.pdf) for geometric and drainage specifications.

## Surface drainage

- **Cross-fall**: The carriageway should have a slope (typically 2–2.5% for bituminous surfaces) so that surface water runs off to the sides and does not pond.
- **Longitudinal gradient**: The road alignment should allow water to flow along the side drains to outfalls.
- **Shoulders** should also drain so that water does not stand against the pavement edge.

## Side drains and culverts

- **Side drains** (open or lined) should be provided to collect and carry runoff. They must be sized for the catchment and kept clear of silt and debris.
- **Culverts** and cross-drains allow water to pass under the road. They should be designed for the design discharge and kept free of blockages.
- **Outfalls**: Drains should discharge to natural streams, stormwater systems, or other designated outlets without causing erosion or flooding.

## Maintenance

- Drains should be **cleaned periodically** (before and during monsoon) so that blockages do not cause overflow onto the road or waterlogging.
- **Repair of damaged drains** and headwalls is part of routine maintenance.

Cross-fall of **2–2.5%** for bituminous surfaces and drain dimensions are specified in [IRC:SP-73](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/irc/irc.gov.in.sp.073.2018.pdf). The date above refers to the relevant IRC guideline revision.
