For pothole and patch repair, both **hot mix** and **cold mix** bituminous materials are used. Choice depends on availability, size of repair, and conditions.

## Hot mix

- **What**: Bituminous mix produced and laid at **high temperature** (e.g. 150–160°C), so the binder is fluid and bonds well.
- **When to use**: **Larger patches**, planned repairs, and where a **lasting repair** is priority. Preferred for structural patches and where traffic can be managed for the short time needed to cool the mat.
- **Pros**: Strong bond, durable, good compaction.
- **Cons**: Needs hot-mix plant and timely transport; not suitable for very small, scattered potholes or emergency fixes in remote locations.

## Cold mix

- **What**: Bituminous mix that can be **stored and applied at ambient temperature** (or with minimal heating). Often used in pre-packed bags or bulk for small repairs.
- **When to use**: **Quick repairs**, small potholes, **emergency** or monsoon patching, and where hot mix is not available or economical.
- **Pros**: No heating on site, easy to store and carry, quick to apply.
- **Cons**: Generally less durable than hot mix; may need early re-treatment or overlay if traffic is heavy.

## Storage and application

- **Hot mix**: Use on the same day; do not store for long. Place and compact while still hot.
- **Cold mix**: Store as per manufacturer (often in a cool, dry place). Apply on clean, dry surface; compact as per specification. Avoid use in standing water.

## IRC and specifications

IRC and state specifications give guidance on **grading**, **binder content**, and **application** for both hot and cold mixes. For durable, large-area repairs, hot mix per IRC is preferred; for small potholes and emergency work, cold mix meeting the relevant code may be used.
